## Business Structure Options for 615 Vinyl (TGGM-Aligned)

### **Option 1: Sole Proprietor + DBA (Current/Simple)**
- **Structure:** Christine operates as sole proprietor under "615 Vinyl" DBA
- **Pros:** Simplest to start, minimal paperwork, full control
- **Cons:** No liability protection, unlimited personal liability, harder to scale
- **Tax Implication:** Schedule C (personal income tax)
- **TGGM Fit:** ❌ Poor – can't formally participate in cooperative structures
- **EIN Required?** Only if hiring employees; otherwise optional (can use SSN)
- **Recommendation:** ⚠️ Transitional only – move to LLC within 12 months

---

### **Option 2: LLC (Recommended for Current Scale)**
- **Structure:** 615 Vinyl LLC (single-member, member = Christine)
- **Pros:** Personal liability protection, professional appearance, flexible taxation
- **Cons:** Annual filing fees ($100–$300/year), slightly more paperwork
- **Tax Implication:** Can choose: taxed as sole prop (default) OR elect S-Corp (if revenue > $60K/year)
- **TGGM Fit:** ✅ Good – LLC can hold assets, enter contracts, join cooperatives formally
- **EIN Required?** YES (even if no employees)
- **Entity Type for TGGM:** Member LLC (Christine holds 100% equity)
- **Recommendation:** ⭐ **Best for 615 Vinyl right now**

**Formation Steps (Tennessee):**
1. File Articles of Organization with TN Secretary of State (~$100)
2. Obtain EIN from IRS (free, online, 5 minutes)
3. Open business bank account (requires EIN)
4. Create Operating Agreement (even if single-member)
5. Register for sales tax (if TN requires for online sales)

---

### **Option 3: C-Corporation (TGGM Growth Model)**
- **Structure:** 615 Vinyl Corp (Christopher could be CEO, board = Christine + advisors)
- **Pros:** Maximum liability protection, professional, scalable, can sell equity/raise capital
- **Cons:** Double taxation (corporate + dividend tax), complex accounting, overkill for current size
- **Tax Implication:** Corporate tax rate (~21% federal) PLUS personal income tax on dividends
- **TGGM Fit:** ✅ Excellent – aligned with TGGM's C-Corp growth vision; can issue equity to TGGM members
- **EIN Required?** YES
- **When to Consider:** When revenue > $250K/year AND planning to scale beyond solo operation
- **Recommendation:** 🔮 Future-state (2–3 years out if growth continues)

**Transition Path to C-Corp:**
- Year 1–2: Run as LLC
- Year 3: If revenue strong + hiring needed → convert LLC to C-Corp (or dissolve LLC, form C-Corp)
- Benefit: TGGM members could invest in 615 Vinyl Corp shares
- Dividend path: Profits can be distributed to TGGM dividend pool

---

### **Option 4: Cooperative/Benefit Corporation (TGGM-Native Model)**
- **Structure:** 615 Vinyl Cooperative (multi-member, members = Christine + TGGM network)
- **Pros:** Aligned with cooperative values, member governance, profit-sharing built-in
- **Cons:** More complex to set up, requires member agreement, slower decisions
- **Tax Implication:** Pass-through (like S-Corp); profits distributed to members pro-rata
- **TGGM Fit:** ✅✅✅ Perfect – native TGGM structure
- **EIN Required?** YES
- **When to Consider:** If Christine wants to scale with TGGM members as co-investors
- **Recommendation:** 🚀 **Strategic long-term option** – can start with LLC, add cooperative layer later

## EIN (Employer Identification Number) Details

**What it is:** A 9-digit ID for your business (like a business SSN)

**When required:**
- If you form an LLC or Corporation (required)
- If you hire employees (required)
- If you operate as partnership (required)
- If optional for sole prop but useful for business credibility

**How to get it:**
- Online at IRS.gov: https://www.irs.gov/ein
- Takes 5 minutes
- Free
- Instant (usually)

**For 615 Vinyl:** Get it now, even as sole prop. Makes scaling easier later.
