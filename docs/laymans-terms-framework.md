# Layman's Terms Framework (7 Primes Model)

**Purpose:** A simplified, plain-English framework for breaking down any business process, product, or system using the 7 Primes schema. Designed to make complex ideas accessible and actionable.

---

## The 7 Primes Explained (Layman's Version)

### **I – Identity** 
*What is this thing? What is its name and core nature?*
- **Simple question:** If you had to explain this to your mom in one sentence, what would you say?
- **Includes:** Name, core function, primary purpose, what makes it *this* and not something else.
- **Example (Order Email):** "A professional message from customer to Christine confirming what they want to buy, when they want it, and what it costs."

---

### **O – Origin**
*Where did it come from? How did it start? What caused it to exist?*
- **Simple question:** Why does this exist? What problem does it solve?
- **Includes:** History, reason for creation, problem it addresses, founding context.
- **Example (Order Email):** "Customers need a clear way to request vinyl designs, and Christine needs to know exactly what they want before she makes it. Email is simple and works for both."

---

### **K – Komposition**
*What is it made of? What are its parts? How are they arranged?*
- **Simple question:** If you took this apart, what would you find inside?
- **Includes:** Components, structure, layers, materials, how pieces fit together.
- **Example (Order Email):** Customer name → Email → Product details → Size/finish choices → Quantity → Total cost → Special notes → Sign-off.

---

### **F – Function**
*What does it do? How does it work? What happens when someone uses it?*
- **Simple question:** What is the step-by-step process? What changes because of this?
- **Includes:** Actions, workflow, cause-and-effect, what gets triggered.
- **Example (Order Email):** 1) Customer adds items to cart. 2) Customer enters name & email. 3) System generates email. 4) Email opens in customer's mail app. 5) Customer reviews and sends. 6) Christine receives and confirms.

---

### **M – Mind (Consciousness / Observer Relevance)**
*Does this thing "think"? Who notices it? What does it mean to different people?*
- **Simple question:** Does this have awareness or intention? How does it make people feel?
- **Includes:** Emotional impact, user perception, meaning, intent, how different people experience it.
- **Example (Order Email):** From customer's view: "I'm ordering something custom I'm excited about." From Christine's view: "I now know exactly what to make and can plan my time."

---

### **R – Relation**
*How does this connect to other things? What is it related to? Where does it sit in the bigger picture?*
- **Simple question:** What does this depend on? What depends on it? What is it part of?
- **Includes:** Dependencies, integrations, ecosystem, upstream/downstream effects.
- **Example (Order Email):** Depends on: cart system, customer data, email service. Feeds into: inventory check, design queue, production timeline, shipping calculation.

---

### **T – Trajectory (Potential / Evolution)**
*Where is this going? How might it change? What could it become?*
- **Simple question:** If this kept improving, what would the ultimate version look like?
- **Includes:** Future state, growth potential, next iteration, long-term evolution.
- **Example (Order Email):** Future: Automatically attach design previews, suggest faster turnaround with rush fee, integrate into customer account dashboard, real-time queue visibility.

---

## How to Use This Framework

### For Business Decisions
1. **Clarity:** Use I/O/K to understand what you're actually doing.
2. **Impact:** Use F/M/R to see what it affects.
3. **Growth:** Use T to plan next versions.

### For Customer Communication
- **I:** "Here's what we're doing for you"
- **O:** "Here's why we're doing it"
- **K:** "Here's how it works"
- **F:** "Here's what happens next"
- **M:** "Here's what it means for you"
- **R:** "Here's how it fits with your other orders"
- **T:** "Here's how we'll make it even better"

### For Problem-Solving
- **Issue in I?** → Confusing brand message
- **Issue in O?** → Doesn't address real customer need
- **Issue in K?** → Process is broken or unclear
- **Issue in F?** → Workflow fails or creates errors
- **Issue in M?** → Customer feels frustrated or unvalued
- **Issue in R?** → Doesn't integrate with other systems
- **Issue in T?** → No path to improvement

```
M – Mind: [How does it make people feel? Who notices it?]
R – Relation: [What is it connected to?]

```

## Application to 615 Vinyl Order Flow

### **Order Email as a 7-Primes Entity**

| Prime | Current State | Better Version |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|
| **I** | Basic transaction confirmation | Professional order request that shows you understand their design vision |
| **O** | Customer needs to tell Christine what they want | Clear handoff from browsing → production planning |
| **K** | Name, email, products, totals | Name, email, product details, customization notes, design reference, timeline preference |
| **F** | Email generated, customer sends it | Email pre-formatted, customer reviews + adds notes, sends, gets tracked |
| **M** | Transactional, feels basic | Feels premium: "This business takes my order seriously" |
| **R** | Standalone email | Connects to: cart, customer profile, production queue, inventory check, shipping system |
| **T** | Same every time | Adapts: includes design preview, shows queue position, offers rush option with pricing |

## Export Block for TGGM Master Index

**615 Vinyl Order Email System – 7 Primes Summary**

- **I:** Professional order request email that captures customer customization preferences and timeline expectations.
- **O:** Enable seamless handoff from browse → production by giving Christine all info she needs; give customer clarity on what they ordered and when to expect it.
- **K:** Customer name, email, product selections (size/finish), quantity, customization notes, budget, preferred timeline, design reference.
- **F:** Customer adds to cart → enters personal info → system auto-drafts professional email → customer reviews/adds notes → sends to Christine → auto-confirmation to customer with tracking setup.
- **M:** Customer feels heard (premiumness); Christine feels prepared (clarity); both feel confident in the exchange.
- **R:** Triggers: inventory check, queue assignment, production calendar update, shipping estimate; depends on: cart system, customer data store, email service.
- **T:** Next: design preview attachment, real-time queue visibility, automatic rush-fee calculation, scheduled production alerts.
